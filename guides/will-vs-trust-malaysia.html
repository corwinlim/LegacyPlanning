Will vs trust in Malaysia: what should you compare?

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Service eligibility, costs and formal applications must be verified independently. The enquiry preview is not accepting submissions.

What is the difference between a will and a trust?

A will generally expresses instructions intended to take effect after death, subject to applicable legal requirements. A trust generally concerns property held and administered by a trustee for beneficiaries under its terms. Their legal treatment, costs and suitability depend on the facts and applicable law.

Does a trust automatically replace a will?

No. The two can serve different purposes, and neither should be treated as universally superior. Ask a qualified adviser about ownership, control, administration, costs, beneficiaries and what happens to assets not covered by an arrangement.

What questions should you ask about costs?

Ask for the written fee schedule, ongoing trustee or administration charges, possible taxes and other costs, cancellation terms and who receives payment. This website does not publish or guarantee service fees.

Who should confirm eligibility?

Formal eligibility, document validity and service availability should be confirmed through qualified advisers and the relevant official processes. Legacy Planning offers general enquiry orientation, not legal advice.
